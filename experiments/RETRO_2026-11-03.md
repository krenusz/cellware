# Retro, 2026-11-03: what we know, what we don't, what we stop doing

One page. The details are in EXPERIMENT_LOG.md and WRITEUP_E5_E25.md.

## Solid (survived pre-registered tests or controls)

1. **Clone fate bias is heritable and strong in culture.** rho_f 0.88 [0.82, 0.92] between
   sister wells (E19). It falls with clone size: 0.95 in small clones, 0.82 in large (E19c).
2. **It is weak in vivo.** Latent r is 0.22 (E19b).
3. **Cells stay undecided until the environment is known.** The learned rule (E10) recovered
   that 71-75% of cells are uncommitted at day 2, from day-4/6 data alone.
4. **Day-2 expression predicts fate beyond library and batch** (B1c: 0.67 leave-one-library-out).
   About half the heritable state is still invisible to scRNA-seq.
5. **The methylation record is neutral.** Clone identity lives in large heterochromatin domains
   and replicates Camellia and EPI-Clone. "Record predicts fate" was a mouse/size confound (B3i).
   The MPP-like methylation axis goes with myeloid bias (B3k), in one dataset only.
6. **The G-CSF vs M-CSF sister agreement (0.51) is real, not sampling** (E20, p < 0.005).
7. **The clones seen under both G-CSF and M-CSF are a fate-selected subset** (B7). The selection
   is already visible in the day-2 state (B9). It is not a sampling artefact (B8, small and of
   opposite sign).
8. **The low G/M presence overlap (0.14) is mostly the design:** about 0.3 founders per well
   (E28, model 0.22).

## Ruled out as the explanation of G-M (each pre-registered, each failed)

Sampling (E20), per-condition gain (E21/E21b), a second trait from within-condition data (E22),
size coupling (E23), pre-split carry-over (E24), fixed clone types (E25), selection with
emergent presence (E26), learned divide/arrest schedules (E27, 0/30 reproduce B7), founder
split (E28). **E29 ceiling test: no.** G-M is capped around 0.15 even with G-M in the loss and a
shared single-cytokine trait up to gamma 5. The cap comes from how every fit reproduces M-CSF
(near-total neutrophil suppression, leaving no clone signal). Reading (a) is now concrete: the
missing structure is clone-specific neutrophil output under M-CSF. B10 tests it in the data.

## The one pattern that keeps coming back

In every model, the better the within-condition fit, the lower the predicted G-M (0.06-0.33).
Starts that reach G-M about 0.5 always break full-M. Two readings are left:
- **(a) A missing structure** that links G and M outcomes but not the full cocktail.
  E29 showed the current family cannot do it (capped around 0.15).
- **(b) The G-M value is partly a chance high** in a 78-clone statistic. E20 puts it below 0.5%
  under the model, so this needs the model to be right, which is circular.

## What went well

- Pre-registration plus a single look kept the held-out shard unseen through nine failures.
  It is still unspent.
- Data-first diagnostics (B7, B9, E27's overlap table) found more than any new model did.
- GitHub Actions made 6-job sweeps take minutes instead of hours.

## What went badly, and what changes

- **Model churn.** Nine simulators in two days, each a small variation. From now on: no new
  simulator without a data diagnostic that it must explain and the current one cannot.
- **Fitting summaries, not structure.** Presence overlap went unnoticed until E27.
  From now on: every model is checked on the structural statistics (presence, overlap, size)
  before the cross statistics.
- **Wasted runs.** E26 ran three times before the fix was actually saved.
  From now on: smoke-test the real script with a short iteration count before dispatching.

## Where the answer probably is

Not in more summary-statistic fitting of this dataset. The question "instructed or selected,
per sister" is answered by **time-lapse of sister pairs split into G-CSF vs M-CSF**: who dies,
who arrests, who switches. That route is not pursued (no outreach). The work continues with
data diagnostics on what we have, following the strongest signal (B10 next).
