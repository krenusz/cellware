## C17 since the last retro, in one paragraph
The parameter table grew to blood 3, pluripotent 2, neural 1. The embryo trees gave the second pluripotent system: H 0.81 [0.53, 0.87] on the train embryos, rising with stage. The CRISPR brain split clones gave the first neural system: P_excess 0.22 [0.17, 0.25], after a CI bug was found and fixed. An exhaustive neural search with a recall gate found 5 candidates; all 5 failed their structure gates, so the neural class stays at 1. The stage-aware memory model (C17-S1T) was read up (H20), pre-registered, passed smoke and power (0.94), and on the fit was **NOT SUPPORTED**: commitment alone explains sister agreement on the embryo trees, and memory adds at most 2%. Two follow-ups ended without new compute: the "memory at commitment near zero" fit had already been done (C5-S2), and an events-vs-divisions test inside CellTag LSK stopped at pre-flight (power 0.29). **No sealed partition was read; every A/B/C is intact.**

## Went right
1. **Every stop came from a rule written before its result.**
   - The C17-S1C S0 gate failed (0 eligible), and amendment 2 was written before its count.
   - The 5 neural gates all failed, and no threshold was relaxed after seeing the counts. G2 was a near miss at 38 of 75.
   - C17-S1T failed its rule at 4 of 7 embryos (6 needed); it was reported as failed and not reframed.
2. **Smoke and power before fits made the fail informative.**
   - C17-S1T had power 0.94 at the planted memory, so "not supported" says something about the trees, not about the test.
   - The split-clone CI bias (bootstrap copies matched themselves) was caught from the method, not from the number. It was fixed with a coverage check (null 100%, planted 100%).
3. **The recall gate made "no more neural sets" credible.** All 8 known sets were re-found before any verdict, and the first, defective run (Zenodo silent, a truncated query) was not used.
4. **Duplicate and feasibility checks ran before compute** (H16, H15). C5-S2 was found before a refit, and the CellTag LSK pre-flight took minutes on clone sizes alone, with nothing sealed touched.
5. **The deep novelty check came before the first script** (H25, lit/novelty/C17-S1T.md). The H20 reading (PATH, quantitative fate mapping, kin correlation) defined the nested reductions that the result then used.

## Went wrong (each lesson lands)

   - The brain split clones' P_excess (0.22) is likely regional identity kept by descent.
   - LARRY's rho_div is memory per division; the gastruloids lose memory per passage.
   - A cross-class law test on these rows would compare unlike quantities. The table does say this, but in free-text caveats, not in a field the test reads.

   -> practice (added to program.md, "Before proposing next steps"): every parameter-table row states its scale (memory per division / memory per event / commitment-dominated / position-dominated), and a cross-system comparison uses rows of one scale only.
   -> proposed (needs Bence's OK): add a `scale` column to c17s1_params.py and make the law-test pre-registration count systems per class *per scale*. Counted that way, no class has 3 systems on the memory-per-division scale today: blood has LARRY only (SIS and CellTag LSK are heritable shares).

## Decisions
- **C17-S1T is done: NOT SUPPORTED (exploratory, train only).** No table row was added. The embryo-tree row keeps its "mostly commitment" caveat.
- "Memory loss is tied to events (passage, commitment), not to division count" stays a cross-system reading. It is untested within any one system: CellTag LSK cannot separate the two (narrow, noisy size proxy; size confounded with commitment).
- **The chapter headline is blocked by systems, not by methods.** The cross-class law test needs 3 systems per class on one scale; today it is pluripotent 2 and neural 1, and no class has 3 on the memory-per-division scale. The pluripotent candidates are in the watch list and the triage below.
