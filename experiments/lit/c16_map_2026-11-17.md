# C16 literature map (2026-11-17)

Bence: "first do a citation and literature map now on these questions". This map serves the C16 questions. It was built from OpenAlex keyword searches (5 question groups, 16 queries, about 170 hits) and the abstracts of the 30 most relevant papers. Abstracts only: no full text was read. Search hits and abstracts are in the session scratchpad and are not saved.

**Exposure note:**
- Colgan et al. 2026 (Cell) is the source paper of the embryo data set (petracer_embryo).
- Only its abstract was read. That is topic level: "fate biases, restriction timing ... the relative contributions of distinct mesodermal origins to endothelium".
- Its result tables and figures cover all 16 embryos, including sealed B and C, so they stay unread (standing rule).

## The questions
- **Q1.** When and how do mesoderm cells become paraxial vs lateral plate? Is it set by time and position of exit from the primitive streak?
- **Q2.** Is fate committed in lineage before it shows in expression?
- **Q3.** Is the family-level bias an inherited cell state or shared position (niche)?
- **Q4.** What are the methods: hidden-state or time-varying transition models on lineage trees?
- **Q5.** What data exist: whole-embryo lineage recordings in mammals?

## Q1: timing and position at the primitive streak (classic fate maps)
| Paper | What it shows | Bearing on C16 |
|---|---|---|
| Lawson 1991, Development (c981) | Single-cell epiblast labelling, E6.7: mesoderm fate depends on where the cell sits in the epiblast and when it passes the streak | Position + time decide germ-layer fate |
| Smith 1994, Dev Dyn (c87, OA) | E7.5 streak dye map: anterior quarter gives paraxial, mid-streak gives lateral plate, posterior gives extraembryonic. **Domains overlap** | The P/L split is mostly *positional*; boundaries are not sharp at E7.5 |
| Kinder 1999, Development (c450) | Order of allocation: heart and cranial mesoderm leave the streak first; **trunk paraxial and lateral mesoderm leave last** | The P/L decision for trunk falls at late streak, around E7.5-E8.0. Matches the C16 gain peaking at E8.0 |
| Ivanovitch 2021, PLoS Biol (c121, OA) | Heart progenitor subsets come from distinct streak regions *and* exit times, with a molecular signature before leaving | Fate is prepatterned in the streak, in both time and space |
| Sweetman 2008, BMC Dev Biol (c85, OA; chick) | Movement of emerging P and L cells is position-dependent; changing their movement did not change fate | Movement and fate can be separated |
| Henrique 2015 (c367, OA); Attardi 2018 (c90, OA) | Bipotent neuromesodermal progenitors keep making paraxial mesoderm from the tailbud through axis elongation | **"After a while everyone knows their place" is only partly true:** the posterior body keeps generating new paraxial cells after E8.5 |
| Sakurai 2005, Stem Cells (c159) | In ES culture, paraxial-like and lateral-like populations **switch** at a measurable rate | Reversibility in vitro. C16 found a near-absorbing bias in vivo, so the two differ |

## Q2: commitment before expression
| Paper | What it shows | Bearing |
|---|---|---|
| **Liu 2026, bioRxiv (DNA Typewriter + FateVec, mouse)** | Fate bias appears in lineage **before morphological diversification at gastrulation**. Loss of potential is progressive and asynchronous. "Cell identity alone predicts neither developmental ancestry nor the timing of fate restriction" | **Closest to Bence's "decide early" idea.** It is published (preprint) at the level of fate bias in time. It does not fit a hidden inherited-state model or test position |
| **Colgan 2026, Cell (data source; abstract only)** | Resolves fate biases and **restriction timing** across 16 embryos | The *timing* question is likely already answered on our own data set. Our angle must be the mechanism (inherited state vs position vs time-varying rates), not the timing |
| Regalado 2025, bioRxiv (read before) | Heritable fate bias in monoclonal gastruloid founders before induction | In-vitro precedent for inherited bias |
| Lin 2026, bioRxiv (in silico lineages) | Heritable "memory genes" infer clonal relations and early fate biases from expression snapshots | Expression *can* carry lineage memory; C16-S2 asks whether it does here |

## Q3: inherited state vs position
| Paper | What it shows | Bearing |
|---|---|---|
| Smith 1994; Lawson 1991 | Fate follows streak position | Position is the textbook explanation |
| Chakrabarti 2018, Nat Commun (c69, OA) | In cultured cells, sister fates were similar and **not explained by proximity**; this pointed to early fate determination | A method template: test the proximity explanation directly |
| Lasrado 2017, Science (c169) | Clonal units organise tissue space (enteric nervous system) | Lineage and position are entangled in vivo |
| Chow 2021, Science (intMEMOIR, c154) | Lineage read out in place, with expression | The kind of data that separates the two directly |
| **Gap** | No paper found that separates inherited state from position for the P/L split in mouse embryos with a tree model | **Open: C16-S3 (position control) would address it** |

## Q4: methods (our model class)
| Paper | Model | Relation to C16 |
|---|---|---|
| Beaulieu 2013, Syst Biol (c412); corHMM | Hidden-rate binary trait model on phylogenies | **C16 M1 is this model class** (hidden rate category with switching), applied to cell trees |
| Hormoz 2016, Cell Systems (c101, OA) | Infers state-transition dynamics from lineage trees plus endpoint single-cell measurements (kin correlations) | Canonical cell-tree precedent for hidden dynamics from sister and cousin correlations |
| Mohammadi 2022, Commun Biol (c18, OA); Nakashima 2020, Bioinformatics (c21, OA) | Tree hidden Markov model; lineage EM for latent states | Same family; used on cultured cells, not whole embryos |
| **Yang 2026, bioRxiv (PASTRI)** | Transition rates from annotated cell trees, **stage-specific** rates from cell pairs at varying tree distance | Very close to the C16-S1 control (rates varying with time). Cite as the method precedent; it has no hidden inherited state |
| Pilarski 2026, PLoS Comput Biol (c5, OA) | CRISPR recordings recover division rates well, but other phylodynamic rates show **biases** | Caveat: tree reconstruction error can mimic or mask clustering. Add a tree-error robustness check before any write-up |

## Q5: whole-embryo mammalian lineage data
- Chan 2019, Nature (c382): first mouse embryo molecular recording.
- Kalhor 2018, Science (c369): whole-mouse homing barcodes.
- Islam 2024, Nature (c47): temporal recording in mouse development.
- Pijuan-Sala 2019, Nature (c1102): reference expression atlas of gastrulation.
- Colgan 2026, Cell: our data source.
- Liu 2026: an independent mouse lineage set (DNA Typewriter). A **possible future replication set**, but it would need H24 sealing before any statistic.
- Gowri 2026, arXiv (lineage inpainting; gap map #25, read): the same atlas, a generative tree model of states. No hidden-state, position or held-out-embryo test.

## What this means for C16
1. **Answer to Bence's question:** the classic fate maps support "the decision is set by time and place of leaving the streak". Trunk P/L cells leave last, at about E7.5-E8.0, which matches the C16 peak. Two caveats from the literature:
   - posterior progenitors keep making new paraxial cells later;
   - in culture the two fates can still switch.
2. **Already published:**
   - fate-bias *timing* in mouse lineage trees (Liu 2026; likely Colgan 2026 on our own data);
   - hidden-rate tree models as a method (Beaulieu 2013, Hormoz 2016);
   - stage-specific rates (PASTRI 2026).
3. **Not found (our possible contribution):**
   - a hidden inherited-state model tested on held-out embryos;
   - the same model tested against time-varying rates (S1) and against **position** (S3);
   - a test of whether the inferred state is readable in expression (S2).

   The position control is the biggest gap and the most direct test of "inherited vs placed".
4. **Robustness to add:** sensitivity to tree reconstruction error (Pilarski 2026), for example re-running S0 after collapsing low-support edges, if the data carry edge support.
