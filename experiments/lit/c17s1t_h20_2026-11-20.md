## 2. Top 5 read (DOIs added to lit/read.txt)
1. **PATH** (Schiffman et al., Nat Genet 2024, doi 10.1038/s41588-024-01920-6, PMC11527590). Phylogenetic
   auto/cross-correlation (Moran's I) on a weight matrix keeping one node or branch distance; z scores by leaf
   permutation or analytic moments. Link to mechanism: for a Markov chain P with limiting distribution pi,
   Phi_t = (P^t)' D P^t; **if reversible**, Phi_t = D P^(2t) and heritability M_ii = (P^2t_ii - pi_i)/(1 - pi_i);
   P is inferred by matrix roots of D^-1 Phi_t (equivalent to kin correlation analysis). Branch lengths (time) scale
   the inference; sparse sampling lowers correlations but inference stays accurate when z > 2.
   **Stated limitation:** transitions Markovian and **state frequencies near equilibrium**. Periodic or
   non-limiting chains break inference. Code public (R package).
2. **Quantitative fate mapping** (Fang et al., Cell 2022, doi 10.1016/j.cell.2022.10.028, PMC9708097). A progenitor
   state is defined by potency only; each state has one **commitment event** with commitment time, population size and
   commitment bias (fractions to daughter states, symmetric or asymmetric division). Commitment time = mean depth of
   "inferred commitment events" (nodes whose two daughters both carry a different state) on a time-scaled tree
   (Phylotime). Accuracy needs sampling fraction >= 25% of a progenitor state. Prolonged commitment windows
   (0.5-1.7 days) and multifurcations are resolved in simulation. **No memory between commitments**: within a state,
   cells are exchangeable; bias is a population fraction, not a heritable per-cell variable. Code public (QFM).
3. **Kin correlation** (Hormoz et al., Cell Syst 2016, doi 10.1016/j.cels.2016.10.015, PMC5142829). C_IJ(u) =
   p_J T^(2u)(I|J) with one transition matrix per division; assumes stationarity. Same algebra as PATH's reversible case.
4. **Memory and relatedness of transcriptional activity** (Phillips et al., Nat Commun 2019,
   doi 10.1038/s41467-019-09189-8). Mother-daughter correlation lambda of 0.86-0.92 per generation, memory of 8-17
   generations, in a self-renewing (stationary) state; no fate or commitment.
5. **Progressive loss of transcriptional memory with differentiation** (BMC Biol 2024,
   doi 10.1186/s12915-024-01846-9). Sister-transcriptome distance (Manhattan) over the first 1-2 divisions, two
   progenitor systems, small n, the key comparison borderline (p about 0.06); no fate readout, no stage curve beyond
   two points.

Not read in full: FateVec preprint (abstract only; no open methods found); listed for the ~26 Nov watch-list check.

## 3. Minimal published mechanism
A stationary Markov chain on cell states with one transition matrix per division (kin correlation, PATH). It predicts
sister agreement that decays geometrically with divisions since the MRCA, the same at every stage. It **cannot**
produce agreement that rises with stage (C17-S1P train embryos: about 0 at the earliest stage to 0.89 at the latest),
because committed states are absorbing (non-reversible, no equilibrium), and PATH lists this as its limitation.
Quantitative fate mapping covers the absorbing part (commitment times and biases) but has no per-division memory
inside a state. No paper read combines the two.

Our minimal model (to pre-register, not fitted yet): per stage s, a transition matrix
P_s = (1 - c_s) [lambda I + (1 - lambda) 1 pi'] + c_s A, with c_s = commitment hazard at stage s, A absorbing
committed states, lambda = per-division memory. Sister agreement after a split at stage s and k divisions follows from
the product of the P matrices along the path, so stationarity is not needed. Nested reductions: c_s = 0 gives PATH/kin
correlation; lambda = 0 gives a pure commitment map (quantitative fate mapping without memory).

## 4. Value
- Already published: stage-rising fate agreement (input only); memory per division in stationary states (Phillips,
  kin correlation, PATH); commitment times from trees (QFM); memory falling with differentiation over 1-2 divisions
  (BMC Biol 2024).
- What ours adds: lambda separated from c_s on the same trees, with a test that the nested reductions fail (c_s = 0
  cannot fit the stage rise; lambda = 0 cannot fit decay with divisions within a stage), and lambda compared across
  systems at matched c_s. It is not a disciplined replication; the nested-model test is the new part. Risk: the
  embryo train trees may not separate lambda from c_s (identifiability), so synthetic recovery comes first.
