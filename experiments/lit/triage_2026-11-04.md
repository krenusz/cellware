## Full-text follow-up (2026-11-04, later)

- **#29 Strasser 2018 Nat Commun (discussion read).** Fitted to time-lapse genealogies, the
  model puts the lineage decision several generations (days) before marker onset. PU.1 changes
  only after the inferred choice, so the PU.1/GATA1 toggle cannot be the decider. Cell cycle is
  about 12 h and similar across lineages. The split studied is GM vs MegE, not G vs M. For us:
  a fate bias set by day 2 and read out at day 4/6 is expected, which fits B21's intra-clone
  correlation. Equal cycle times weaken lineage-specific proliferation as a default.
- **#4 Hu, Qiu & Dong 2015 JBC (discussion read).** A G-CSFR Y729F mutant prolongs Erk1/2 and
  switches G-CSF output to monocytes; blocking Erk (or c-Fos/Egr1) restores neutrophils. M-CSF
  gives strong, sustained Erk in Lin- marrow, and Erk inhibitors under M-CSF cut monocytes and
  **raise the absolute number of neutrophils**. That points to redirection (instruction), not
  only death of monocyte precursors. Caveats in the paper itself: monocytes are not fully
  blocked, and "some Lin- cells are already committed to the monocyte lineage". Bulk cultures,
  so clone-level selection is not excluded. For us: a second, independent line supporting partial
  M-CSF instruction (with #26), consistent with E40's small M instruction term. G-CSF itself
  gives transient Erk, which fits a permissive G signal (#22, #37).

## Shared by Bence, 2026-11-05: Manicka & Levin, "A topoietic model of collective morphogenesis and compartmentalization" (bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.11.03.763900, posted 2026-11-04)

Abstract and discussion read (PDF; no HTML full text yet). Xenopus embryos survive a teratogen
more often in larger groups (0% alone to 92% at N = 306; Tung, Sperry et al. 2024). Model: each
embryo constitutively secretes a stress signal (ATP/adenosine) that diffuses and decays. The
interior field rises with group size because volume outgrows the absorbing surface. The field
sets survival (forcing it directly confirms the direction), forms before fate commitment, and
reconstitutes after shuffling. The governing quantity is group size over the signal's decay
length. Predictions act on the medium: less degradation or added ATP rescues sub-critical
groups; elongated or single-file groups rescue less at the same number. Limitation, stated by
the authors: the molecular chain (receptor, kinase, how the teratogen triggers release) is a
plausibility case, not identified.

**Relevance to cellware.**
1. *Original question (shared policy, local signals, tissue).* This is a direct worked example of
   a collective outcome set by a diffusible signal plus geometry, with no cell-level
   instruction. It is a good reference for the tissue line, not the LARRY line.
2. *LARRY / E45.* A culture-level analogue would be a community (Allee-type) effect: clone or
   well survival depending on local density through a shared secreted signal. In our data, B27
   found that clone presence does not rise with the number of sampled day-2 cells (G 0.17 / 0.14 /
   0.18). That is weak evidence against a strong per-clone Allee effect, since day-2 sampling is
   a poor founder proxy. Wells in the LARRY design share a medium within a condition, so a
   well-level density effect would look like a condition effect and cannot be separated from
   the cytokine with these data. No new test is proposed now.
